A reflection from the Principal Upanishads

I know.
So why don't I?

The mind knows the right thing to do — and still the body won't move. The Upanishads have a precise answer: knowing and doing live in different parts of you.

00 — The gap

Knowing is not doing.

“My mind knows what's the right thing to do. Still, somehow, it gets difficult to perform those actions.”

THE INTELLECTI know ✓study · apply · build
the gap
THE BODYI don't do ✗scroll · delay · later

This is one of the oldest problems people have written about, and the Upanishads don't treat it as a lack of willpower or intelligence. They treat it as a question of structure: who in you knows, who in you acts, and what connects them. Five passages answer it from five angles. Every Sanskrit line and translation below is quoted exactly from the project's corpus; the sections marked “my reading” are interpretation.

ना

You're in good company: Nārada knew everything, and was still in sorrow

In the Chāndogya, the sage Nārada lists every science he has mastered — and admits it hasn't helped. Sanatkumāra leads him to the boundless.” The teacher's answer to him is the chain you'll find in chapter 04.

01 — The structure Kaṭha Upaniṣad 1.3.3–9

You are a chariot.

Five parts, each with a job. Knowing belongs to one of them. Moving belongs to another.

The verses

आत्मानँ रथिनं विद्धि शरीरँ रथमेव तु ।
बुद्धिं तु सारथिं विद्धि मनः प्रग्रहमेव च ॥

ātmānam̐ rathinaṃ viddhi śarīram̐ rathameva tu ।
buddhiṃ tu sārathiṃ viddhi manaḥ pragrahameva ca ॥

“Know the Self to be the master of the chariot, and the body to be the chariot. Know the intellect to be the charioteer, and the mind to be the reins.”

इन्द्रियाणि हयानाहुर्विषयाँ स्तेषु गोचरान् ।
आत्मेन्द्रियमनोयुक्तं भोक्तेत्याहुर्मनीषिणः ॥

indriyāṇi hayānāhurviṣayām̐ steṣu gocarān ।
ātmendriyamanoyuktaṃ bhoktetyāhurmanīṣiṇaḥ ॥

“The senses they speak of as the horses; the objects within their view, the way. When the Self is yoked with the mind and the senses, the wise call It the enjoyer.”
Kaṭha Upaniṣad 1.3.3–4tr. Vidyavachaspati V. Panoli

यस्त्वविज्ञानवान्भवत्ययुक्तेन मनसा सदा ।
तस्येन्द्रियाण्यवश्यानि दुष्टाश्वा इव सारथेः ॥

“But whoso is devoid of discrimination and is possessed of a mind ever uncollected – his senses are uncontrollable like the vicious horses of a driver.”
Kaṭha 1.3.5
Reins held

यस्तु विज्ञानवान्भवति युक्तेन मनसा सदा ।
तस्येन्द्रियाणि वश्यानि सदश्वा इव सारथेः ॥

“But whoso is discriminative and possessed of a mind ever collected – his senses are controllable like the good horses of a driver.”
Kaṭha 1.3.6

What it says to you · my reading

When you say “my mind knows”, the Kaṭha would say: your charioteer knows. The intellect sees the road perfectly. But the chariot doesn't move by the charioteer seeing — it moves by the reins, and the reins are the mind, which has to be “ever collected” (yuktena manasā sadā). Notice the word sadā — always. Not once, when you feel inspired.

So the problem isn't that you don't know enough. More knowing only improves the charioteer's map. What's slack is the reins — and reins are tightened by practice, not by information.

Where it leads

विज्ञानसारथिर्यस्तु मनः प्रग्रहवान्नरः ।
सोऽध्वनः पारमाप्नोति तद्विष्णोः परमं पदम् ॥

“But the man who has a discriminating intellect as his driver, and a controlled-mind as the reins, reaches the end of the path – that supreme state of Vishnu.”
Kaṭha 1.3.9tr. Vidyavachaspati V. Panoli
02 — The default Kaṭha Upaniṣad 2.1.1

The horses face outward.

The pull isn't a flaw in you. The text says the senses were made this way.

The verse

पराञ्चि खानि व्यतृणत् स्वयम्भूस्तस्मात्पराङ्पश्यति नान्तरात्मन् ।
कश्चिद्धीरः प्रत्यगात्मानमैक्षदावृत्तचक्षुरमृतत्वमिच्छन् ॥

parāñci khāni vyatṛṇat svayambhūstasmātparāṅpaśyati nāntarātman ।
kaściddhīraḥ pratyagātmānamaikṣadāvṛttacakṣuramṛtatvamicchan ॥

“The self-existent damned the out-going senses. Therefore one sees externally and not the internal Self. Someone (who is) intelligent, with his eyes turned away, desirous of immortality, sees the inner Self.”
Kaṭha Upaniṣad 2.1.1 · graph: DIAGNOSES “Pleasure that scrolls on”tr. Vidyavachaspati V. Panoli

What it says to you · my reading

The first thing this verse removes is shame. Looking outward is the default setting, not a personal defect: every sense opens outward, and most of what's on your phone is designed to keep them that way. You were never going to out-will that by knowing better.

The second thing it gives is a direction: someone steady, “with his eyes turned away”, looks within. Practically, that starts with the outward pulls you can simply remove — before the moment of choice, not during it.

03 — The engine Chāndogya 7.22.1 · 7.23.1

You only act where happiness is.

The Chāndogya says something blunt: nobody acts without getting happiness from it. Follow that through, and the gap explains itself.

The rule

यदा वै सुखं लभते ऽथ करोति ।
नासुखं लब्ध्वा करोति ।
सुखम् एव लब्ध्वा करोति ।
सुखं त्व् एव विजिज्ञासितव्यम् इति ।
सुखं भगवो विजिज्ञास इति ॥

“‘When one obtains happiness’, then alone does one act. Without obtaining happiness one does not act. Only on obtaining happiness does one act. But one must desire to understand happiness’. ‘Revered sir, I desire to understand happiness’.”
Chāndogya 7.22.1tr. Swami Swahananda
The real payment

यो वै भूमा तत् सुखम् ।
नाल्पे सुखम् अस्ति ।
भूमैव सुखम् ।
भूमा त्व् एव विजिज्ञासितव्य इति ।
भूमानं भगवो विजिज्ञास इति ॥

“That which is infinite, is alone happiness. There is no happiness in anything finite. The infinite alone is happiness. But one must desire to understand the infinite’. ‘Revered sir, I desire to understand the infinite’.”
Chāndogya 7.23.1tr. Swami Swahananda

What it says to you · my reading

The pleasant choice (preyas) pays happiness immediately; the good choice (śreyas) pays later. At the moment of choosing, the mind follows 7.22.1 exactly: it acts where happiness is — which, right now, is the pleasant.

The fix isn't to stop wanting happiness — the text doesn't ask that. It's to find a deeper happiness in the right action itself: the boundless, 7.23.1, rather than the small. In practice: what this hour of work makes possible beyond today — independence, the people you'll help, becoming someone who can do the thing. When the good action carries that, it can compete.

What most of us expect
  1. Understand it
  2. Feel steady & motivated
  3. Then act
→ waits forever for the feeling
What the Chāndogya says
  1. Find the happiness in it
  2. Act
  3. Steadfastness follows
  4. Faith, reflection, understanding

What it says to you · my reading

This is the most practical verse in the whole page: “Only on acting does one become steadfast.” You have been waiting to feel steady before acting. The Chāndogya puts it the other way round — the steadiness is produced by acting. Two minutes of real work produces a little niṣṭhā; tomorrow's two minutes start from there.

Notice, too, where the chain bottoms out: in happiness (sukha) — the same rule as chapter 03. The two chapters are one idea: find the happiness in the action, act, and the steadiness you were waiting for arrives behind you.

The verse

… यथाकारी यथाचारी तथा भवति । साधुकारी साधुर् भवति । पापकारी पापो भवति । पुण्यः पुण्येन कर्मणा पापः पापेन । अथो खल्व् आहुः । काममय एवायं पुरुष इति । स यथाकामो भवति तत्क्रतुर् भवति । यत्क्रतुर् भवति तत् कर्म कुरुते । यत् कर्म कुरुते तद् अभिसंपद्यते ॥

… yathākārī yathācārī tathā bhavati । sādhukārī sādhur bhavati । pāpakārī pāpo bhavati । puṇyaḥ puṇyena karmaṇā pāpaḥ pāpena । atho khalv āhuḥ । kāmamaya evāyaṃ puruṣa iti । sa yathākāmo bhavati tatkratur bhavati । yatkratur bhavati tat karma kurute । yat karma kurute tad abhisaṃpadyate ॥

“As it does and acts, so it becomes; by doing good it becomes good, and by doing evil it becomes evil – it becomes virtuous through good acts and vicious through evil acts. Others, however, say, ‘The self is identified with desire alone. What it desires, it resolves; what it resolves, it works out; and what it works out, it attains.’”
Bṛhadāraṇyaka 4.4.5 (closing lines) · graph: TEACHES Kāma, Karmatr. Swami Madhavananda

What it says to you · my reading

Two lines matter. First: “As it does and acts, so it becomes.” You don't become a person who studies daily by deciding to be one; you become it by doing it. Second, the view the verse reports: desire → resolve → work → attainment. A “should” you don't actually want never turns into resolve.

So don't only argue with yourself about what's right. Spend time making the right thing wanted: picture the specific life it leads to, talk to someone already living it, notice small wins. That is how knowledge enters the chain.

    07 — The practice

    The next time you hesitate.

    Six steps for the moment the horses pull, each from one of the verses above. Step through it once, so it's familiar when you need it.

    Step 1 of 7

    ŚVETĀŚVATARA 2.9 · EXPECT RESTIVE HORSES

    प्राणान् प्रपीड्येह स युक्तचेष्टः क्षीने प्राणे नासिकयोच्छ्वसीत ।
    दुष्टाश्वयुक्तम् इव वाहम् एनं विद्वान् मनो धारयेताप्रमत्तः ॥

    Controlling the senses with an effort, and regulating the activities in the body, one should breathe out through the nostrils when the vital activities become gentle. Then the knowing one, without being in the least distracted, should keep his hold on the mind as on the reins attached to restive horses.

    Don't wait for a calm mind. Expect the pull daily and hold on anyway — “without being in the least distracted”.

    MUṆḌAKA 3.2.4 · BUILD STRENGTH

    नायमात्मा बलहीनेन लभ्यो
    न च प्रमादात्तपसो वाप्यलिङ्गात् ।
    एतैरुपायैर्यतते यस्तु विद्वांस्तस्यैष आत्मा विशते ब्रह्मधाम ॥

    This Self is not attained by one devoid of strength, nor through delusion, nor through knowledge unassociated with monasticism. But the Self of that knower, who strives through these means, enters into the abode that is Brahman.

    Sleep, food and movement are part of the practice. Exhausted horses can't be steered.

    KAṬHA 1.3.13 · THE PAUSE

    यच्छेद्वाङ्मनसी प्राज्ञस्तद्यच्छेज्ज्ञान आत्मनि ।
    ज्ञानमात्मनि महति नियच्छेत्तद्यच्छेच्छान्त आत्मनि ॥

    Let the wise man merge speech in his mind, merge that (mind) into the intelligent self and the intelligent self into the Mahat. (Let him then) merge the Mahat into the peaceful Self.

    A one-minute settling before acting: stop the inner talk, let the mind come to rest, then decide from the clear place.
