A day well spent

One universal template for an ordinary modern day, built from what the Upaniṣads say about waking, food, work, speech, people and sleep. Set your wake time, follow the arc, fill the worksheet at night.

    Balance in all aspects of life

    Feed all five layers of yourself, every day

    The Taittirīya describes a person as five selves, each inside the other: made of food, of breath, of mind, of understanding, and of joy. A day that feeds only one (work, say, or pleasure) leaves the others hungry, and you feel it as tiredness, restlessness or emptiness by evening. Give each layer its small daily share and the day balances itself.

    यतो वाचो निवर्तन्ते । अप्राप्य मनसा सह । आनन्दं ब्रह्मणो विद्वान् । न बिभेति कदाचनेति ।

    One is not subjected to fear at any time if one knows the Bliss that is Brahman, failing to reach which words, along with the mind, turn back.
    Taittirīya 2.4 · tr. Swami Gambhirananda

      Avoiding bad habits

      Every pull is a choice between the pleasant and the good

      The Kaṭha says both arrive together, many times a day. You don't beat a habit by willpower in the moment; you see it, name it, and have the better action already chosen.

      श्रेयश्च प्रेयश्च मनुष्यमेतः तौ सम्परीत्य विविनक्ति धीरः ।
      श्रेयो हि धीरोऽभि प्रेयसो वृणीते प्रेयो मन्दो योगक्षेमाद्वृणीते ॥

      śreyaśca preyaśca manuṣyametaḥ tau samparītya vivinakti dhīraḥ ।

      The preferable and the pleasurable approach man. The intelligent one examines both and separates them. Yea, the intelligent one prefers the preferable to the pleasurable, whereas the ignorant one selects the pleasurable for the sake of yoga and kshema.
      Kaṭha 1.2.2 · tr. Vidyavachaspati V. Panoli

      Staying in control during the day

      Three pauses: at sunrise, at noon, at sunset

      Kauṣītaki stood before the sun three times a day: as it rose, at its height, and as it set, asking each time that the faults of the day be taken away. Keep the same three appointments in a modern form. Each takes two to ten minutes.

      The all-conquering Kausitaki indeed was wont to worship the rising sun … In the same way he was (wont to worship the sun) when it was in the mid-heaven … In the same way … when it was setting … Whatever sin he committed by day or by night that it takes away.
      Kauṣītaki 2.7 (abridged) · tr. Dr. A. G. Krishna Warrier

      Sunrise: set the reins

      1. Sit still, eyes closed, ten slow breaths.
      2. Say or think: from the unreal to the real, from darkness to light.
      3. Write one intention and three essentials.

      Noon: be the watching bird

      1. Stop for two minutes before lunch.
      2. Ask: is my mind clear, restless or dull?
      3. Ask: am I still on my three essentials? Correct course.

      Sunset: put the work down

      1. Close the laptop with a done list and tomorrow's first step.
      2. Walk or sit outside for ten minutes without the phone.
      3. Let the day's work go; the evening belongs to people.

      द्वा सुपर्णा सयुजा सखाया समानं वृक्षं परिषस्वजाते ।
      तयोरन्यः पिप्पलं स्वाद्वत्त्यनश्नन्नन्यो अभिचाकशीति ॥

      Two birds that are ever associated and have similar names, cling to the same tree. Of these, one eats the fruit of divergent tastes, and the other looks on without eating.
      Muṇḍaka 3.1.1 · tr. Swami Gambhirananda · at each pause, step from the eating bird into the watching one

      Ending the day

      Sleep is the falcon going home

      Screens off an hour before bed. Close the worksheet, forgive the day's slips, and lie down as something that has finished its flight, wanting nothing more from today.

      तद् यथास्मिन्न् आकाशे श्येनो वा सुपर्णो वा विपरिपत्य श्रान्तः संहत्य पक्षौ संलयायैव ध्रियते । एवम् एवायं पुरुष एतस्मा अन्ताय धवति । यत्र सुप्तो न कं चन कामं कामयते न कं चन स्वप्नं पश्यति ॥

      As a hawk or a falcon flying in the sky becomes tired, and stretching its wings, is bound for its nest, so does this infinite being run for this state, where, falling asleep, he craves no desire and sees no dream.
      Bṛhadāraṇyaka 4.3.19 · tr. Swami Madhavananda
